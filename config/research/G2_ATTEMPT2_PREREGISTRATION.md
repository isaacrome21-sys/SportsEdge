## 3. Dispersion treatment (C2 — answers G-CAL)

**What changes.** The simulation draws for the **offensive-TD leg** and the **made-FG leg** become negative-binomial with a per-leg dispersion parameter θ_leg. Rare legs (def/ST TD, safety) remain Poisson draws at their §2.4 rates; conversion sampling is unchanged. (Corroboration that this vocabulary is native to the project: the frozen comparison grid's recorded family name is `B_INDEPENDENT_NB_PLUS_MARGIN_SPIKES_0_3_7` — NB dispersion plus explicit key spikes; DIAGNOSIS G-CAL.)

**Parameterization (frozen).** For a leg with post-latent mean μ′ (the attempt-1 construction: leg mean × shared latent draw, §2.5) and dispersion θ_leg:
- Variance = μ′ + μ′²/θ_leg.
- Draw = NumPy `negative_binomial(n = θ_leg, p = θ_leg / (θ_leg + μ′))` for finite θ_leg; θ_leg = ∞ is exactly the attempt-1 Poisson draw `poisson(μ′)`, so the attempt-1 form is inside the search space and CV may honestly return to it.
- Period legs: the per-quarter TD_q and FG_q legs each carry their own θ, selected identically (design draft §7: C2 applies to period legs identically).

**Selection (frozen).** θ_leg is selected **per fold, per leg, inside the training window only**, by expanding-season CV: for each candidate θ in the frozen grid **{5, 10, 20, 50, 200, ∞}**, score = mean over held-out training-season games of the NB log-pmf of the realized leg count under the leg model's fitted mean for that game (the GLM/EB mean fit does not involve θ; θ enters only the predictive pmf and the simulation draw). Select the θ maximizing the CV score; **ties break to the larger θ** (least dispersion — the conservative direction). Selection never touches the validation fold being scored.

**What is NOT introduced.** No recalibration of outputs: no Platt/isotonic/temperature adjustment of the simulated PMF, no post-simulation reshaping of any kind outside the frozen K3 component of §4. The distribution must be right by construction (§6 of the design draft). G-CAL's measurement method is carried from attempt 1 verbatim (§5.5).

## 4. The K3 key-number component (ruling K3 — full pre-fitting specification)

### 4.1 Status and scope

Owner ruling K3 amends the family's input discipline to permit **one** fitted key-mass component, specified here in full **before any fitting**. The component is part of the candidate model form. It applies to the **full-game joint distribution only** — period PMFs are *not* key-adjusted (G-KEY is a full-game gate; extending the component to periods would be an unruled change). It is estimated **inside each training fold**, from that fold's training window only, by the frozen estimator of §4.3, and applied **uniformly to every game in the fold** as part of the model's distribution construction, **before any gate is computed**. The pre-component ("skeleton") PMF is preserved and reported alongside the candidate PMF at execution, so the component's effect is fully auditable (§7).

### 4.2 Construction (frozen)

For game *g* in fold *f*, let S_g be the skeleton joint PMF over (home_score, away_score) from the 50,000 simulated paths (post-dispersion, post-latent, pre-key-component), and let S_g(m) be its margin law, m = home − away. The candidate joint PMF P_g is S_g with the margin law transformed at the keys, preserving the skeleton's conditional distribution of score pairs within each margin value:

- **Keys (frozen):** K = {−7, −3, +3, +7} — exactly the G-KEY keys. Margin 0 is deliberately **not** a component key: it is not measured by the gate, and no unmeasured freedom is added.
- **Source windows (frozen, disjoint):** W_−7 = {−9, −8, −6, −5}; W_−3 = {−4, −2, −1}; W_+3 = {1, 2, 4}; W_+7 = {5, 6, 8, 9}. (Every source margin is within 2 points of its key; the windows partition the near-key margins; margin 0 is never a source.)
- **Transfer:** for each key k with fold parameter λ_{f,k} (§4.3): every score pair with margin m ∈ W_k has its probability multiplied by (1 − λ_{f,k}); the removed mass, λ_{f,k} · S_g(W_k), is added to margin k, distributed over the game's skeleton score pairs at margin exactly k **in proportion to their skeleton probabilities**. Total probability is conserved exactly. The within-margin conditional composition of every margin value is the skeleton's — the component moves mass *between adjacent margin values only*, never reshapes score pairs within a margin.
- **Corner rule (frozen):** if a game's skeleton mass at key k is exactly 0 while its window mass is positive, no transfer occurs for that (game, key) — λ is effectively 0 there. (With 50,000 paths and attempt-1 skeleton key masses ≈ 0.027–0.028, this corner is not expected to occur in scored folds; it is frozen anyway so no implementation discretion exists.)

### 4.3 Estimator (frozen, closed form, training-window only)

Per fold f and key k:
1. Fit the fold's count model (all legs, §§2–3) on the training window T_f.
2. Simulate the **training games themselves** with the fitted model — 50,000 paths per training game, same child-seed rule — yielding per-training-game skeleton margin PMFs. (These in-sample simulations are used **only** for this estimation; they are never scored.)
3. Let S̄_f(k) = the mean over training games of the per-game skeleton P(margin = k); S̄_f(W_k) likewise for the window; R_f(k) = the realized fraction of training games with final margin exactly k (training outcomes — training data, legitimately fitted).
4. **λ̂_{f,k} = clip( (R_f(k) − S̄_f(k)) / S̄_f(W_k), 0, λ_max )**, with **λ_max = 0.50** frozen. If S̄_f(W_k) = 0, λ̂_{f,k} = 0.
5. **Bounds/priors, stated plainly:** λ_{f,k} ∈ [0, 0.50] per key per fold. The floor at 0 makes the component **one-directional**: it can only add key mass toward the training window's realized level, never remove key mass (if the skeleton over-holds a key on training data, the component does nothing at that key). The cap bounds the total transferable share of any window. There are no other parameters: no per-game λ, no dependence on the game's own skeleton beyond the proportional transfer of §4.2, no fitted window or cap at execution time.
6. λ̂ values for every fold and key are recorded in the fit artifact at execution (§7), before the fold's evaluation games are simulated in the runner's execution order — estimation strictly precedes evaluation simulation within each fold.

### 4.4 Relationship to the gates

G-KEY (§5.6) is computed on the **candidate** (post-component) PMF, exactly as attempt 1 computed it on its candidate PMF; the skeleton's key errors are computed identically and reported as diagnostics, so the record shows what the component did and what the skeleton alone would have scored. All other gates (G-JOINT, G-CAL, and the rest) also consume the candidate PMF: there is **one** candidate distribution — using different distributions for different gates would be incoherent. The standard does not move: ≤ **0.0075** at each key, verbatim (§5.6).

### 4.5 What makes this component INVALID (attempt-voiding violations)

The following are **attempt-voiding violations** — if any occurs, the attempt is void regardless of any gate outcome and the violation is recorded in the ledger. **Consumption (Amendment 2 reconciliation):** a violation (or any contamination) discovered **after** a completed scored run **voids the result** — recorded `VOID` in the ledger — but the attempt **counts as consumed**: voiding annuls the verdict, it never refunds the budget, and no re-run under attempt 2 is permitted for any reason. A violation discovered **before** scoring aborts the run as **NOT_RUN** (attempt not consumed), with the disposition recorded in the ledger:

1. **Any use of evaluation-fold data in λ estimation** — evaluation games' outcomes, margins, or simulated PMFs influencing λ̂ in any way.
2. **Any post-scoring adjustment** — applying, re-applying, or altering the component (or any probability reshaping) after any gate or scoring output for a fold has been produced; the component exists only inside the frozen distribution construction, upstream of all scoring.
3. **Any change after freeze** to the keys, windows, cap λ_max, the estimator formula, or the corner rule.
4. **Application to any baseline** (intercept, league-rate, league empirical PMF, comparator (ii′)'s inputs other than through the candidate's own marginals as §5.3 defines, or any prospective comparator) — the component is part of the candidate model only.
5. **Selective application** — applying the component to some games and not others within a fold for any reason other than the frozen corner rule, or choosing λ per game.
6. **Any second reshaping stage** — no additional redistribution, spike, or calibration transform of the candidate PMF beyond §3's dispersion (in the draws) and this component.

Attempt 1's forbidden-input list entry "post-hoc key-number redistribution" remains in force for everything outside this section: what K3 permits is exactly and only the fitted, frozen, training-window-estimated component specified above.

### 5.4 G-JOINT — standard verbatim; comparator leg (ii) replaced per ruling J1

- **Leg (ii′) — NEW, per J1 (replaces the frozen discrete_v2 leg at development):** the **independent-marginals composition of the candidate's own candidate PMF**: per game, take the home and away marginal PMFs of the game's candidate joint PMF (post-component, §4) and compose them independently, P_(ii′)(h, a) = P_g^H(h) · P_g^A(a); score realized scores under the composed PMF with the same floor rule. *Why this comparator:* it is closed-form from the candidate's own path set — nothing is fitted inside the attempt, nothing external is required, nothing leaks — and it tests exactly what a joint gate should test at development: whether the joint law (dependence structure included) beats its own marginals composed independently. It is runnable in the development window by construction. **Governed limitation (owner, issue #2131): (ii′) is derived from the candidate's own PMF — it benchmarks the value of the dependence structure only and is NOT an independent external benchmark; no external benchmark is evaluated in the development window, and the development record must not present (ii′) as one.**
